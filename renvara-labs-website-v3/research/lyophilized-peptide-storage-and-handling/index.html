Lyophilized peptide storage in the lab

General laboratory practice for keeping dry, freeze-dried research peptides stable, with sources. Storage only.

Shop the catalog (opens in a new tab)Open the COA library

By Renvara Labs. Published 2026-10-10. Sources are listed at the end. For laboratory research use only.

The short version

Dry, freeze-dried peptides are generally stored cold, dry, tightly closed and away from bright light. A common recommendation is -20 degrees C or colder, and letting a vial reach room temperature before it is opened to limit moisture uptake. This guide covers storing the dry material only. Follow the label and any safety data sheet for your lot.

Temperature, light and moisture

Show the list: Temperature, light and moisture
  • Temperature. Laboratory suppliers advise storing lyophilized peptides at -20 degrees C, with colder, dry conditions preferred for longer storage. Stability depends on the sequence, and most lyophilized peptides are reported to last for years under these conditions.
  • Light. Keep vials away from bright light.
  • Moisture. Peptides can absorb water from the air. Peptides rich in aspartic acid, glutamic acid, lysine, arginine or histidine are the most prone, and a desiccator with a tightly capped vial is the usual precaution.
  • Before opening. Let the closed vial warm to room temperature first, then reseal it tightly. Opening a cold vial lets moisture condense on the powder.

Sequences that need extra care

Cysteine, methionine and tryptophan residues are prone to oxidation, so peptides containing them are usually stored with limited exposure to air. Check the sequence of the compound you hold, not only the product name.

Labels, lot records and security

Renvara’s Terms ask laboratory buyers to retain lot identification, keep materials stored safely and out of reach of children, animals and unauthorized persons, and to avoid using any material with compromised packaging, unclear identity or a suspected quality problem until the issue is resolved. Keeping the lot number with the vial is what lets you match it to a public report later.
