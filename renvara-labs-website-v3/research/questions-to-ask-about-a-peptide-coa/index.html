8 questions to ask about any peptide COA

A buyer’s checklist for any supplier’s certificate, applied to Renvara’s own six public reports.

Shop the catalog (opens in a new tab)Open the COA library

By Renvara Labs. Published 2026-10-10. Sources are listed at the end. For laboratory research use only.

The short version

Eight questions work on any supplier’s certificate: does it name your lot, who issued it, which methods it lists, whether blends are split by component, whether a quantity sits beside the label, when it was analyzed, whether it claims more than it tests, and what happens when your lot is missing. Each is answered below for Renvara’s own six reports.

Eight questions, answered for Renvara’s six public reports

Show the list: Eight questions, answered for Renvara’s six public reports

  1. Does the report name the lot on my vial?

    Why it matters. A report describes one lot. If the lot number is missing or different, it does not describe your vial.

    Renvara’s public reports. Each of the six public reports names a lot (for example DPS-9368773 for the BPC-157 + TB-500 blend). Only one lot per product is public.

  2. Who issued it, and can I open it on their own site?

    Why it matters. A report you can open on the issuing laboratory’s site is easier to trust than a file hosted only by the seller.

    Renvara’s public reports. All six open as PDFs hosted by Horizon Analytical. The reports opened in the audit do not state the laboratory’s city or state.

  3. Which methods are listed?

    Why it matters. The method decides what the result can show. Chromatography speaks to purity, mass spectrometry to identity.

    Renvara’s public reports. The library lists UPLC/MS purity, identity and quantitation for all six.

  4. Are blends reported per component?

    Why it matters. A blend should show a purity and quantity for each peptide, not one combined number.

    Renvara’s public reports. Yes for the BPC-157 + TB-500 blend (two lines) and GLOW (three lines).

  5. Is a measured quantity shown next to the labeled strength?

    Why it matters. Purity alone does not tell you how much target material was found.

    Renvara’s public reports. Yes. For example, GHK-Cu lists 49.1 mg measured against a 50 mg label.

  6. Is the analyzed date shown?

    Why it matters. The date tells you when the sample was tested, which helps you judge whether the report belongs with the lot you hold.

    Renvara’s public reports. All six show 2026-09-16.

  7. Does the report claim more than it tests?

    Why it matters. A purity result is not an endotoxin, sterility, metals or solvents result.

    Renvara’s public reports. Renvara states a seven-point program, but the public reports list UPLC/MS only. This site does not claim any lot passed all seven.

  8. What happens if my lot is not listed?

    Why it matters. A supplier should tell you which documentation exists for your lot and how to get it.

    Renvara’s public reports. Renvara’s library says to email support with the product, lot and order number. See the verify page.

Put it to work

The same checks fit on one page. Print the 60-second COA checklist or look up a lot with the verify page. Where the answer is “not published”, that is stated plainly, because a clear gap is more useful than a confident guess.
