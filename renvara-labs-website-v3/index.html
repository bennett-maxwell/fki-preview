1/7HPLC / UPLC

HPLC / UPLCPurity and related impurities

What it measures. Liquid chromatography separates the components of a sample as it passes through a column. A detector, commonly UV absorbance, records each component as a peak. Purity is usually reported as the main peak's area as a percentage of the total peak area. UPLC applies the same principle with smaller particles and higher pressure for sharper separations.

Why it matters. It shows how much of the signal comes from the intended peptide and how much from related impurities such as truncated or modified sequences. It cannot see water, salts or other species that do not absorb at the detection wavelength, so a purity percentage is not the share of the powder that is peptide.

How to read it. Find the method and detection wavelength, the chromatogram, the retention time of the main peak and the reported area percent. Then compare the product name and lot number on the report with your vial.

Reference: USP general chapter <621> Chromatography (opens in a new tab)

Mass spectrometryMolecular mass and identity

What it measures. A mass spectrometer measures the mass-to-charge ratio of ions made from the sample. For peptides, several charge states of the same molecule often appear, and software converts them into one observed molecular mass.

Why it matters. Comparing the observed mass with the expected mass supports confirmation of the compound's identity. A match does not separate two peptides that share a mass but differ in sequence, which is why sequence-level evidence is a separate test.

How to read it. Look for the expected mass, the observed mass, the stated tolerance and the ionization method named in the report.

Reference: USP general chapter <736> Mass Spectrometry (opens in a new tab)

Identity / sequenceIntended peptide confirmation

What it measures. Identity analysis asks whether the material is the intended peptide. The depth depends on the method: fragmentation mass spectrometry (MS/MS) can read part or all of a sequence, while amino acid analysis checks composition rather than order.

Why it matters. Mass alone leaves room for sequence isomers. Sequence-level evidence narrows that gap, and the level of confirmation depends on the method used.

How to read it. Check which method the report names, whether it states sequence coverage, and whether the result is a statement such as 'conforms' or a data table you can inspect.

LAL / BET endotoxinBacterial endotoxins

What it measures. Bacterial endotoxins are lipopolysaccharides from the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria. The Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) test mixes the sample with a lysate reagent that reacts to endotoxin. Gel-clot, turbidimetric and chromogenic readouts are the three technique families.

Why it matters. Endotoxin can carry through from manufacturing steps and water, and it can interfere with cell-based in vitro assays even when the peptide itself is pure by chromatography.

How to read it. Results are given in endotoxin units (EU) per milligram or per milliliter, with the limit and the method stated. A purity report does not contain this result.

Reference: USP general chapter <85> Bacterial Endotoxins Test (opens in a new tab)

Elemental impuritiesHeavy-metal screening

What it measures. Elemental analysis, usually by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) or optical emission (ICP-OES), measures trace metals. Renvara's description names lead, arsenic, cadmium and mercury, the four Class 1 elements in the USP and ICH framework.

Why it matters. Metals can enter from catalysts, reagents, equipment or containers, and they can affect assay chemistry. A purity percentage does not report them.

How to read it. Look for element-by-element results with units (often micrograms per gram) beside stated limits. For a copper-containing compound such as GHK-Cu, copper is part of the molecule, so read any copper result with that in mind.

Reference: USP general chapters <232> and <233> and ICH Q3D (opens in a new tab)

Residual solventsManufacturing-solvent screening

What it measures. Residual-solvent analysis, commonly headspace gas chromatography, detects organic solvents that can remain after synthesis and purification.

Why it matters. ICH Q3C groups solvents into three classes: those to be avoided, those to be limited and those with low toxic potential. Leftover solvent can also change how a material behaves in an assay.

How to read it. Check which solvents were screened, the result for each, the limit and the units (usually parts per million).

Reference: USP general chapter <467> Residual Solvents and ICH Q3C (opens in a new tab)

Sterility testingViable microbial contamination

What it measures. Sterility testing looks for viable bacteria and fungi in the sample under the conditions of the test. Typical approaches are membrane filtration or direct inoculation into growth media, followed by incubation and observation. USP <71> calls for incubation of not less than 14 days.

Why it matters. Contaminating microbes can overgrow or confound cell-culture work. A sterility result applies only to the sample that was tested.

How to read it. Look for the method, the media, the incubation period and a statement such as 'no growth observed'. Do not infer a sterility result from a purity report.

Reference: USP general chapter <71> Sterility Tests (opens in a new tab)

Quick answers.

Which report applies to my vial?

Use the report with the same product, strength and lot number as your vial. A report for a different lot does not describe the lot you received. The COA library lists six public lot reports as of 2026-10-10.

What do the public COAs cover today?

The six public reports in the COA library are listed as UPLC/MS purity, identity and quantitation, all analyzed on 2026-09-16 by Horizon Analytical. They are not endotoxin, sterility, elemental-impurity or residual-solvent reports.

Does every lot pass all seven tests?

Renvara states that its seven-point program is performed in Utah. The public COAs do not show all seven results for any lot, so this site does not claim that a lot passed all seven. Ask support for the documentation that exists for your lot.

How much is shipping?

For U.S. orders: DHL Ground is $12.99 under $150 and free at $150 and above. FedEx 2Day is $19.99 under $150, $6.99 from $150 to $199.99, and free at $200 and above. Next Day is $32.99 at every order value.
