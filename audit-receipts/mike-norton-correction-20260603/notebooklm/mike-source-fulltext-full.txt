SECTION 2 — THE GAP: WHERE YOUR CONNECTIONS ARE LEAKING
Let's name the gap plainly, Mike, because you named it for us. When we asked what is actually slowing you down and costing you, you listed six things, and they are not random — they are a chain. Here they are, in your words: missed calls, slow lead response, appointment booking, admin work, scheduling, and marketing content. Read as a group, they tell one story: the connections are coming in, and too many of them are leaking out before they ever reach a conversation with you.
Start with the two that hurt the most, because they are the front door: missed calls and slow lead response. You are getting fifty leads a month, Mike, but a lead is not a client — a lead is a moment. It is a window that is open right now and closing fast. When a call comes in and you are mid-session with another client, mid-build, mid-thought, that call goes unanswered. And an unanswered call from a prospect is very often a permanent goodbye. They do not leave a voicemail and patiently wait. They call the next person. This is the part that should sting a little, given your whole mission: a missed call is, by definition, a missed connection. The exact thing Origins promises to eliminate for your clients is happening to you, at your own front door.
Then there's slow lead response — and this is where your own five-minute instinct turns out to be backed by hard research. There is a well-known study out of the Harvard Business Review, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," which found that companies that contact a new lead within an hour are about seven times more likely to have a meaningful conversation with a decision-maker than those who wait even an hour longer, and dramatically more likely than those who wait a day (Harvard Business Review, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," 2011). Five minutes is the gold standard for a reason, Mike. Your instinct is correct. The problem is not that you don't know the right speed — it's that a human being running an entire business solo physically cannot hit five minutes every time. You sleep. You take meetings. You eat. The window keeps opening when you are not at the keyboard, and every minute that passes, that connection cools.
Now the second cluster: appointment booking and scheduling. Even when you do catch a lead, the conversation often dies in the back-and-forth. "Does Tuesday work? No? How about Thursday at two? Actually I have a conflict." Every round trip is another chance for that warm lead to go cold or get distracted. Booking friction quietly kills deals that were ready to close.
And the third cluster: admin work and marketing content. This is the slow, invisible tax. The notes, the recaps, the CRM updates, the follow-up emails, the posts you keep meaning to write to amplify your frequency — your words again — but never quite get to because you are buried in the operational sludge. Every hour you spend on admin is an hour you are not spending in the high-value work only you can do: the actual wisdom, the actual strategy, the actual connection. For a solo founder chasing a revenue goal, that misallocation of your own time is one of the most expensive things in the entire business, and it does not even show up on an invoice.
That is the gap, Mike. Demand is arriving. Your front door is sometimes unattended. Your booking has friction. And your own hours are being eaten by work that does not need your specific genius. None of this is a character flaw — it is simply what happens when one person is the entire system. The good news is that this is exactly the shape of problem AI agents were built to solve.

SECTION 3 — THE AI AGENT TEAM ADVAITA WOULD BUILD FOR YOU
Here's the reframe, Mike. You don't need to become a bigger company to fix this. You need a team — but the team is software. Advaita builds AI agents that act like dedicated staff: they answer, they respond, they book, they follow up, they write, and they never sleep, never take a lunch, and never miss a call. And the design principle behind all of it is your own principle turned around to serve you: never miss a connection. Let's walk through the team we'd build, mapped directly to the six things you told us are slowing you down, and wired into the tools you already use — ClickUp, Slack, Notion, and Google Drive — so nothing here means ripping out your stack.
First, the voice and missed-call agent. This is your front door, finally staffed twenty-four hours a day. When a call comes in and you cannot pick up, the agent does — it answers in a natural voice, greets the caller, understands why they're reaching out, captures their details, and either books them or hands the warm context straight to you. No more voicemail black hole. No more "I'll call them back later" that becomes "I forgot." For someone whose product is literally about never missing a connection, Mike, this is the agent that makes your own house match your own message.
Second, the speed-to-lead agent, set to your number: five minutes. The moment a lead comes in from any channel, this agent responds — a real, personal, on-brand reply — inside that five-minute window, every single time, day or night. Remember the Harvard research: contacting a lead fast multiplies your odds of an actual conversation. You already wanted five minutes. This agent just makes five minutes the floor instead of the lucky exception. With fifty leads a month, the difference between "responded fast" and "responded eventually" is not a rounding error — it's deals.
Third, the booking and scheduling agent. Once a lead is engaged, this agent closes the loop to a calendar slot without the ping-pong. It offers your real availability, confirms the time, sends the invite, and reminds them so they actually show. The back-and-forth that used to kill momentum disappears. This directly retires two of your six: appointment booking and scheduling.
Fourth, the admin automation agent, wired into ClickUp, Notion, and Drive. After a call, it drafts the recap, updates the record, creates the follow-up tasks, and files the notes where they belong — in the systems you already live in. The invisible tax gets paid by software instead of by your evenings. You get your hours back for the work that actually requires you.
Fifth, the content engine. You want to amplify your frequency — that's your phrase — but content is the thing that always slips when you're busy. This agent drafts your posts, your emails, your follow-up sequences, in your voice, on a steady cadence, so your presence in the market stays consistent even in the weeks you're heads-down delivering. It turns "I should post more" into a system that just runs.
Sixth, the follow-up agent — the quiet revenue multiplier. Most leads don't close on the first touch; they close on the fifth, the seventh, the gentle nudge weeks later. A solo founder almost never has the bandwidth to run disciplined long-tail follow-up, so those leads simply evaporate. This agent remembers everyone, follows up on schedule, and resurrects connections you'd otherwise lose. It is, very literally, a never-miss-a-connection machine for the back half of your pipeline.
Notice the through-line, Mike. Every one of these six agents exists to make sure a connection that wanted to reach you actually does — at the door, in the first five minutes, on the calendar, in the follow-up. We are building, for you, the exact thing you build for your clients. And we're building it on top of ClickUp, Slack, Notion, and Drive, so it strengthens the workflow you already have instead of replacing it.

SECTION 4 — THE REALISTIC ROI: WHAT THIS IS ACTUALLY WORTH TO YOU
Let's be honest and conservative about the money, Mike, because you told us your goal is revenue and we are not going to insult you with fantasy math. We don't know your exact numbers, and we are not going to pretend we do. But we can reason carefully from the one number you gave us: roughly fifty leads a month.
Here is the simplest, most defensible way to think about it. Right now, some portion of those fifty leads never reach a real conversation with you — they hit a missed call, they get a slow response and go cold, or they fall out of a clunky booking flow. You don't need to recover all of them to change your year. You just need to recover a few. Imagine the agents help you have a genuine conversation with even two or three additional leads a month that you'd otherwise have lost to silence or delay. Over a year, that's a couple dozen extra real conversations — at the very top of your funnel — that simply didn't happen before. For a consultant, conversations are the raw material of revenue. A small, steady lift in conversations compounds into a meaningfully different revenue line over twelve months, without you working more hours. In fact, while working fewer.
And that's the second half of the return, which matters just as much for a solo founder: your time. McKinsey's research on automation has consistently found that a large share of the day-to-day activities people perform — by their estimates roughly 60 to 70 percent of activities across many roles — could be automated with current technology, and that knowledge work is full of exactly the repeatable, rules-based tasks that AI handles well (McKinsey Global Institute, "A Future That Works: Automation, Employment, and Productivity," 2017). For you, that's the admin, the scheduling, the recaps, the first-draft content. Every hour the agents take off your plate is an hour you can redeploy into the actual high-value work only you can do — or simply not work. Both are worth real money to a founder who is currently the entire team.
So the ROI story for you is deliberately modest and, we'd argue, conservative: recover a handful of leads that are currently leaking out, and reclaim a meaningful chunk of your week from admin. We are not promising you a 10x overnight, Mike. We're promising something more durable — a front door that's never unattended, a five-minute response that's automatic instead of heroic, and your own time handed back to you. For a business at your stage, with your revenue goal, that's not a luxury. That's the unlock.
