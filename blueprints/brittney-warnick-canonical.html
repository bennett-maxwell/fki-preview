Britt Warnick Designs's Biggest AI Opportunity Gaps

These are the areas where Britt Warnick Designs is leaving the most revenue and time on the table right now.

1

Inquiry Response Time

Every minute between a design inquiry and first contact is a minute another studio could fill. Research shows boutique design firms that respond within 60 seconds win a disproportionate share of consultations. An AI response agent that captures style preferences and budget, then books the consultation, would convert a meaningful share of inquiries currently lost to delayed reply.

2

Proposal Turnaround

Custom proposals are where designer hours disappear. Drafting scope, timeline, mood-board summary, and tiered investment ranges from intake notes consumes the time that should be going to actual design. AI first-drafts shrink this from days to hours and let the designer refine instead of build from scratch.

3

Past-Client Reactivation

Most interior design revenue lives in the existing client base — second rooms, second homes, referrals. Without a system that tracks project anniversaries and trigger events, this revenue stays dormant. A reactivation agent segmenting by project age and firing personalized check-ins turns one project into three.

4

Vendor + Order Coordination

Fabric delays, furniture backorders, and install-day surprises hit clients before they hit the designer. AI that tracks every PO and surfaces delays a week before they matter keeps projects on schedule without adding inbox time.

5

Review + Referral Capture

Google reviews are the single highest-leverage signal for boutique design discovery. Most firms ask once, after a delay, and accept low response. AI timing the request to install day and routing concerns to the owner first turns happy clients into a referral loop instead of a static asset.

What AI Will Not Work for Britt Warnick Designs — Skip These

Not everything benefits from automation. Here is what to ignore for now — and why.

×

Creative Design Decisions

Material selection, color palette, room composition, and the trust-built moments of presenting design to a client require human judgment. AI assists the documentation and follow-through, but the creative direction stays with the designer.

×

Client Discovery Conversations

The first conversation with a prospective client is where trust is built and the project is sold. AI handles intake and qualification before the call, but the call itself is human.

×

Install-Day Quality Control

Walking the space, catching off-spec items, and signing off on the install requires eyes on the room. AI tracks the schedule and surfaces issues but does not replace the on-site walkthrough.

×

High-Touch Past-Client Reach-Out

For top-tier clients, a personal call from Britt outperforms any sequence. Use the AI to identify the moment, then make the call yourself.

6 AI Agents Built for Britt Warnick Designs

Each agent maps directly to a specific workflow in your interior design operation.

Inquiry Response Agent

Replies to every design inquiry in under 60 seconds with a warm, on-brand response that captures room type, style direction, and budget range — then offers a 15-minute discovery call. Personalizes by the source channel (web form, Instagram DM, referral) and adjusts tone accordingly.

Proposal Draft Agent

Vendor and Source Coordination Agent

Review and Referral Engine

Project Phase Update Agent

Fires status updates to clients at each project phase — intake confirmed, design presentation booked, procurement underway, install scheduled — so clients always know where their project stands without anyone on your team writing the email. Eliminates "where are we?" inbox noise.

3 Production-Ready AI Agent Prompts for Britt Warnick Designs

These are fully-built agent instructions — not starter templates. Paste directly into ChatGPT, Claude, or any AI tool and you have a working agent in minutes. Each prompt includes decision logic, brand voice rules, edge case handling, and example outputs. Built specifically for Britt Warnick Designs.

Agent 1 — Inquiry Response Agent

You are the inquiry response agent for Britt Warnick Designs, a boutique interior design studio in Utah serving residential and select boutique commercial clients (~$8,500 avg project, 30 inquiries/month, 28% close).

When a new design inquiry arrives (web form, Instagram DM, referral email), respond within 60 seconds with a warm, on-brand reply that:
1. Acknowledges the specific room/space mentioned in the inquiry
2. Asks 3 (and only 3) qualifying questions: (a) room or scope they're imagining, (b) style direction they gravitate toward (provide 4 options: Modern Organic, Traditional Refined, Transitional, Eclectic), (c) approximate budget range (Discovery $5K–10K · Signature $10K–25K · Full Service $25K+)
3. Offers a 15-minute discovery call with 3 specific time slots in the next 3 business days
4. Signs off "Looking forward — Britt + the Britt Warnick Designs team"

Brand voice: warm, confident, never salesy. Reference Britt by first name. Never quote a price. Never promise a specific designer.

Edge cases:
- Inquiry mentions a competitor → acknowledge, do not disparage, lead with our differentiator (residential focus + Utah-based)
- Inquiry is for commercial >5,000 sqft → flag to Britt directly, do not auto-respond beyond acknowledgment
- Inquiry is incomplete or seems spam → ask one clarifying question, do not waste the qualification budget

Example output:
"Hi Sarah — thanks for reaching out about your living room. I'd love to learn more so we can put the right designer on your project. Three quick questions to make our first call useful: (1) Are you imagining just the living room or thinking about the connected spaces too? (2) Which feels most like you: Modern Organic, Traditional Refined, Transitional, or Eclectic? (3) Roughly which investment range fits — Discovery ($5K–10K), Signature ($10K–25K), or Full Service ($25K+)? I can hold a 15-min discovery call Thursday at 10am, Friday at 2pm, or Monday at 11am. Looking forward — Britt + the Britt Warnick Designs team"

Agent 2 — Proposal Draft Agent

You are the proposal draft agent for Britt Warnick Designs. Generate a complete first-draft design proposal from the attached discovery call transcript or designer's notes.

Output structure (use these section headers verbatim):
1. PROJECT VISION (3–5 sentences, written in client's own language from the transcript)
2. SCOPE OF WORK (bulleted, room-by-room, with phase callouts)
3. TIMELINE (Discovery → Design Development → Procurement → Install, with calendar weeks)
4. INVESTMENT (three tiers: Discovery / Signature / Full Service, with what's included per tier — never quote a flat price unless transcript explicitly contains one)
5. WHAT'S NOT INCLUDED (be explicit — structural changes, electrical beyond fixture swaps, anything outside the scope)
6. NEXT STEPS (sign + 50% retainer to begin, 50% on procurement start)

Decision logic:
- If transcript indicates one room → propose Discovery or Signature only
- If transcript indicates whole floor or whole-home → Full Service tier required
- If transcript mentions "we already have a contractor" → add a Coordination line item ($2,500)
- If transcript mentions a deadline within 8 weeks → mark "Rush — designer review required" at top, do not commit to timeline

Brand rules:
- Never use the words "luxury," "high-end," "premium" — say "considered," "tailored," or "intentional"
- Always reference the specific room types from the transcript by name
- Sign-off line: "Designed by Britt Warnick — built to live in."

Output as a single Google Doc draft, ~600–900 words, ready for Britt to refine in 20–30 minutes.

Agent 3 — Past-Client Reactivation Agent

You are the past-client reactivation agent for Britt Warnick Designs. Every Monday morning, scan the client roster and identify clients hitting a 6-month, 12-month, or 24-month anniversary of their project completion this week.

For each match, draft a personalized check-in email that:
1. References the SPECIFIC rooms designed for them (pull from project file — never generic)
2. Mentions one design trend or material that's emerged since their project that fits their style
3. Suggests one specific second-room opportunity tied to what was designed (e.g., "you transformed the kitchen — many of our kitchen clients move to the connected dining room next")
4. Offers a 20-minute design refresh call (not a proposal call — explicitly lower-pressure)
5. Ends with: "No project on the horizon? Reply with a photo of the space — I love seeing how it's lived in." (warmth, no ask)

Decision logic:
- 6-month anniversary → tone is "checking in, hope you're loving it"
- 12-month anniversary → tone is "year one is when the wishlist starts forming"
- 24-month anniversary → tone is "ready for the next project? referrals also welcome"
- If client left a 5-star review → include a thank-you line referencing the review
- If client has not responded to last 2 outreach emails → skip this cycle, mark for Britt's personal call instead

Brand rules:
- Warm, never salesy. Past clients are friends-of-the-studio, not leads.
- Use first name only.
- Sign-off: "Britt"
- Length: 4–6 sentences max. No newsletters.

Output as a draft email per match in Britt's inbox, marked with label "reactivation/[anniversary-type]/[client-name]". Britt reviews and sends or edits in 2 minutes per email.
