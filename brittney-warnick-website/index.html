Studio Capabilities

Britt Warnick Designs is a Salt Lake City studio specializing in luxury residential interiors, vacation-home renovations, and boutique commercial spaces. Every project starts with a discovery conversation, a measured site walk, and a written design language statement before a single sample board gets pulled. We do not work from mood boards alone. The studio operates on a simple principle: the home or property has to function for the way you actually live, not the way an aspirational lifestyle would have you live. That conviction shapes every choice from the floor plan revisions to the door pulls.

Full-service interior design

From foundation-up new builds to single-room refreshes, the studio runs end-to-end: design language, space planning, FF&E sourcing, custom millwork, installation oversight, and final styling. We are not a decorator pulling pillows. We are an integrated design-build advisor working alongside your architect and general contractor from schematic through punch list.

Renovations and remodels

Kitchen, primary bath, and whole-home renovations with general contractor coordination. The studio maintains a vetted network of finish carpenters, plasterers, lighting installers, and stone fabricators across the Wasatch Front. We coordinate trade sequencing, lead-time tracking, and inspection scheduling so the homeowner never has to chase the project themselves.

Vacation properties

Park City and Wasatch Back specialty: mountain modern, lodge revival, and ski-in/ski-out short-term rental design that performs on Airbnb and VRBO while feeling like a home, not a rental. The brief is always dual: must hold its value to the family for years, must rent at the top quartile of comparable listings the rest of the time.

Commercial and hospitality

Boutique retail buildouts, spa interiors, and small hospitality projects. Recent work includes a 2,400 sf medical spa in Sugarhouse and an 1,800 sf cafe interior in Holladay. Commercial work is structured the same way as residential at this studio: the brand, the staff workflow, and the customer journey shape every design decision before aesthetics enter the conversation.

Procurement and trade management

All FF&E procured at trade pricing. The studio absorbs the back-office burden of dozens of vendor accounts, lead-time tracking, freight and receiving coordination, and white-glove installation. Clients receive a single weekly procurement report so they always know where every piece is in the pipeline.

Sourcing philosophy

We blend antique and custom with strategic high-end production pieces. Sourcing trips to Round Top, Brimfield, and Paris when budgets allow. Custom millwork through a network of Utah cabinetmakers we have used for years. Stone and tile through direct relationships with Italian and Spanish quarries via a Salt Lake importer who also serves the Sundance and Deer Valley projects in the area.

Featured Projects

Heber Valley Modern Farmhouse

5,400 sf primary residence on a working ranch. The brief: a contemporary interior that respects the wood-and-stone vernacular of the valley without becoming a Pinterest pastiche. Project highlights include reclaimed oak floors from a deconstructed Idaho barn, a custom soapstone island that took six months to source and fabricate, hand-troweled lime plaster walls in the great room with a soft warm undertone, and antique European lighting fixtures sourced over an 18-month period through trade dealers in Belgium and Provence. The kitchen pantry doubles as a coffee-and-cocktail bar with brass-mesh cabinet inserts and a hidden ice maker. The primary bath features a stone trough sink milled from a single block of Utah-quarried sandstone.

Cottonwood Heights Hillside Remodel

A 1970s daylight basement transformed into a full mother-in-law suite plus a wine library. Reframed two structural walls, raised the basement floor 14 inches to walk-out the back, and built a 600-bottle climate-controlled wine library with a tasting island fabricated from a single slab of Calacatta Viola marble. The mother-in-law suite includes a compact kitchen, a sitting room with a gas fireplace, and a private deck overlooking the canyon. The connection between the new basement and the main floor was reworked so that the staircase reads as an architectural feature rather than a utilitarian transition.

Promontory Cabin (Park City)

3,200 sf ski-in/ski-out cabin used as both a family retreat and a high-end short-term rental. Custom millwork bunk room sleeps eight in pairs of twins stacked vertically with privacy curtains and individual reading lamps. Mudroom with heated floors and seating for ten ski-boot changes plus dedicated cubbies for each family member. Kitchen designed for catering crews on rental weeks: two dishwashers, a separate prep sink, and a coffee station that doubles as a check-in counter for incoming guests. The cabin has booked into the top decile of Promontory rental properties since opening.

Sugarhouse Medspa Buildout

2,400 sf medical aesthetics space. Six treatment rooms, a relaxation lounge, and a discreet aftercare exit so post-procedure clients do not have to walk past arrivals. Material palette: warm white oak, brushed brass, hand-glazed Moroccan tile in the lounge, and quiet acoustic finishes in every treatment room. Wayfinding designed so first-time clients can self-navigate without staff handholding. Owner reports a measurable lift in average ticket value within the first ninety days of operating in the new space.

Liberty Wells Bungalow Refresh

1920s bungalow whose plan was preserved but whose surfaces had been deferred-maintenance for thirty years. The studio kept every original window, restored the original plaster ceilings, refinished the existing fir floors, and replaced cabinetry, lighting, plumbing, and finishes throughout. The result is a home that reads as a thoughtful contemporary update of its 1920s bones rather than a flip. Total square footage 1,650.

Holladay Mountain Modern

New construction 6,800 sf primary residence with views to Mount Olympus and Lone Peak. The interior brief was Scandinavian-mountain-modern: warmer than pure Scandinavian, more restrained than typical mountain modern. White oak floors with a wire-brushed finish, ceramics by a Utah maker, custom blackened-steel hood, and an exterior-grade Burmese teak ceiling running from the entry porch through the great room as a continuous visual line.

Avenues Carriage House

A detached carriage house converted into an artist studio for an established Salt Lake painter. Specific requirements: 11-foot ceilings, a north-facing skylight, a wet bench for cleaning brushes, and a quiet sleeping loft so the artist could nap mid-day if needed. The studio is currently used four days a week and has been featured in a regional design publication.

FAQ

How long does a typical residential project take from contract to install?

Full-service whole-home design: 10–14 months from contract to install day for a 4,000–6,000 sf primary residence. Renovations: 6–9 months depending on permitting and lead times. Single-room refreshes: 8–12 weeks. We provide a detailed week-by-week project timeline at the proposal stage so you know exactly what to expect at each phase.

What is your typical project investment?

Full-service primary residences typically run a specific per-square-foot range in FF&E plus design fee combined. Vacation properties trend differently because rental performance is part of the brief. We provide written investment ranges after the discovery walk so there are no surprises once the project begins, and we structure the agreement with phase-based payment milestones tied to deliverable handoffs.

Do you work with my architect and builder, or do you bring your own?

Both. We have a vetted bench of architects and general contractors across the Wasatch Front whom we love working with and whose work we know intimately. If you already have a team in place we plug into their cadence and contribute interior design leadership without disrupting a project that is already in motion. If you are starting from a blank piece of land we can recommend a builder and architect we have worked with on similar projects.

Will you take on out-of-state projects?

Selectively. We have completed projects in Jackson Hole, Sun Valley, and Coeur d Alene. The studio has a structured remote workflow for projects outside the Wasatch Front including monthly site visits during the construction phase, weekly video calls with the GC, and a shared project portal where the homeowner sees every selection and approval in one place.

How do you handle FF&E procurement?

All FF&E is procured through the studio at trade pricing. Clients see the trade discount on every invoice — we do not mark up product. The studio earns a coordinated procurement fee per project that is outlined in the proposal. Many clients tell us this transparency is what made them comfortable with the engagement structure from the first proposal.

Do you offer hourly consults for clients who do not want full design?

We accept a limited number of hourly consults each month for clients who want strategic guidance on a single decision — a renovation scope, a paint and material direction, a one-room refresh — without engaging the full design service. These are typically two-to-four-hour engagements with a written summary and a curated short list of follow-on recommendations.

What does the discovery process look like?

A discovery engagement is the first chargeable step of any project. It includes a measured walk of the home or property, a series of conversations with the household decision-makers about how they actually live, a written design language statement summarizing the direction, and a phase-and-fee proposal for the full project. Most clients tell us the discovery alone surfaces design questions and household priorities they had not realized they had not aligned on.

How do you decide whether a project is the right fit?

We do not take projects where we sense the homeowner and the studio are not aligned on aesthetic direction or working style. Every engagement starts with a no-cost call to understand the project, the budget, the timeline, and the values driving the decision. If the studio is not the right fit we will say so and recommend two or three peer designers we trust.

Where can I see more work?

Featured projects appear on Instagram and in periodic publication features. The studio also keeps a private portfolio that is shared with prospective clients after the initial call.

How does the AI Blueprint factor in?

Britt has invested in an internal AI Blueprint that helps the studio handle discovery scheduling, vendor lead-time tracking, and aftercare in a way that frees the designer to focus on the design work itself. The Blueprint runs in the background and is not a chatbot the client interacts with — it is the operational layer that keeps the project moving.

Design Process

The studio works in five phases — Discovery, Schematic, Development, Documentation, and Installation. Each phase ends with a written milestone deliverable and a homeowner decision gate. Nothing moves to the next phase until the current phase has been approved in writing. This is the discipline that keeps the project on budget and on schedule.

  1. Discovery — site walk, conversations, design language statement, phase-and-fee proposal.
  2. Schematic — space planning, room-by-room functional plans, sample boards by room, material direction.
  3. Development — final selections, custom millwork drawings, lighting plan, plumbing fixture schedule.
  4. Documentation — bid set for the GC, specs for the trades, sample binder for the field.
  5. Installation — staged delivery, white-glove receiving, final styling, photography, punch list.
