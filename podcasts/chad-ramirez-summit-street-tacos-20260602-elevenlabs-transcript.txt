Hi Chad, welcome. This walkthrough was built for you and Summit Street Tacos, from what you told us.

You are already running a strong restaurant operation. You have three Denver-area locations, a team of twenty-five, real catering demand, active local marketing, and a brand that can grow if the handoffs around each store get tighter. The goal of this AI roadmap is not to replace the team you have built. The goal is to make sure your team gets a system that answers quickly, follows up consistently, and gives you a clean operating picture without you chasing every detail.

The first place AI helps you is catering response. When a catering inquiry comes in during lunch rush, your managers may be serving guests, solving staffing questions, or handling inventory. The buyer still expects a clear answer. Your Catering Speed-to-Lead Agent can reply in under sixty seconds, collect event date, headcount, pickup or delivery needs, budget range, dietary notes, and preferred store, then route the next action to the right manager. You keep the human judgment. The AI handles the first complete response so the buyer does not keep shopping.

The second place AI helps you is guest recovery. Reviews, direct messages, and contact forms can spread across too many channels. Your Guest Recovery Agent can review each message, classify urgency, draft a warm response in your voice, and flag anything sensitive for your approval. You get faster guest care without asking managers to remember every platform after a long shift.

The third place AI helps you is manager reporting. You have more than one location, which means more than one version of the day. Your Manager Shift Summary Agent can collect sales notes, labor issues, catering opportunities, guest incidents, inventory alerts, and open tasks, then send you one owner-ready summary. You can make decisions from one clean view instead of pulling updates from five tools.

Your current stack can stay in place. GoHighLevel keeps the contact and pipeline record. Slack and ClickUp keep team communication and tasks moving. Google Drive keeps documents organized. Your point-of-sale and online ordering tools keep store activity flowing. Your AI layer sits on top of those tools and handles the repeatable work between them. Nothing gets ripped out. Everything gets connected around the moments where time and attention usually leak.

Here is what the first agent would feel like in a real day. A customer asks about catering for an office lunch. The AI answers with a short, friendly message, asks for date, headcount, delivery or pickup, location, dietary needs, and budget range. It tags the inquiry as catering, stores the details in GoHighLevel, and gives the manager a clean next step. If the buyer does not reply, the follow-up continues politely. If the buyer is ready, the manager gets the summary instead of a half-complete message thread.

Here is what the second agent would feel like. A guest leaves a review saying the order was late or missing an item. The AI classifies the issue, drafts a response that sounds like Summit Street Tacos, and recommends whether the message should be reviewed by you before it goes out. You avoid canned replies. You also avoid silence. The system helps you respond with speed and care.

Here is what the third agent would feel like. At the end of the day, each store sends notes in whatever format the manager used. The AI turns those notes into one dashboard: sales blockers, staffing needs, catering leads, guest feedback, inventory risks, and next actions. You can see what needs attention tomorrow without reading a pile of scattered updates.

For your franchise inquiry path, the rule is even more important: qualify carefully, never send legal documents automatically, and never make earnings claims. Your Franchise Inquiry Qualification Agent can collect identity, target market, available capital range, restaurant experience, timeline, and why the brand fits them. You get a summary that helps you decide if a conversation is worth your time, while the system keeps the legal boundary clean.

The financial side should stay realistic. This roadmap does not promise a specific dollar outcome. The calculator on your Blueprint lets you enter your own guest ticket, catering value, close rate, lead volume, and time savings. That keeps the math honest. For a fast-casual restaurant group, the most credible value often comes from faster catering response, better guest recovery, less owner follow-up, and stronger repeat guest communication. Those wins compound because they happen every day.

Your first thirty days would be simple. In days one through three, the intake map gets built. We define what counts as catering, guest recovery, manager reporting, franchise inquiry, local marketing, and owner dashboard activity. In days four through seven, the first automations go live in controlled mode. Your team reviews output before anything sensitive goes out. By day thirty, the repeatable paths are running: inquiries are captured, follow-ups are tracked, summaries are clean, and you are using the system to make decisions instead of chasing updates.

The mobile experience matters because your guests and catering buyers are not always sitting at a desk. The apply form, Blueprint, podcast player, and qualifier need to work on a phone. The page should load cleanly, the audio should have play, pause, skip, seek, and speed controls, and the qualifier should submit before any next-step scheduling path opens. That is the customer journey we are testing here.

The next step for you is the qualifier. It is not a pressure call. It is a short fit check that updates the same CRM contact already created by your Blueprint form. You answer the questions, the system records the submission, and only then can the next step open. That protects tracking, keeps the contact record clean, and prevents a calendar bypass.

If you only use one idea from this roadmap, start with catering speed-to-lead. That is the easiest first win because it is specific, measurable, and tied to revenue. If you use two ideas, add guest recovery so public feedback gets a faster, better response. If you use three ideas, add the manager shift summary so you get owner visibility across every location.
