Your first helper is a speed to lead agent. When a fresh Meta or Facebook lead arrives, it answers in seconds, asks the simple qualifying questions, and offers two clear windows for a free in home water test. It keeps the message short. It never quotes a price. It never promises a system before the test. It just gets the homeowner from interest to a booked conversation while the lead is still warm.

Your second helper is an aged lead reactivation agent. You have old leads that still have value because they already raised their hand. This helper works those leads in small waves, not one giant blast. It reminds the homeowner why they cared about water quality, offers a fresh water test, and stops immediately if someone opts out. Every reply gets logged so your callers know who is worth calling next.

Your third helper protects appointments. It confirms the booked water test, reminds the homeowner the day before, sends a morning reminder, and helps reschedule when needed. That matters because a missed appointment wastes ad spend, caller time, and technician time. You should measure booked appointments, showed appointments, no shows, and reschedules each week so the system earns trust with numbers.

Your fourth helper follows up on open quotes. After a technician gives a real quote, this helper can send approved follow up messages that reference the homeowner's concern, explain the value in plain language, and mention financing when that is allowed. It never invents pricing. It only uses the quoted amount or routes the question back to a person. That protects trust while keeping warm deals from going quiet.

Your fifth helper asks for reviews and referrals after clean installs. If the customer is happy and no issue is open, it asks for a Google review and then asks whether a neighbor or family member has similar hard water concerns. If there is a complaint, refund question, or service issue, it does not ask for a review. It routes the issue to the team first. That simple rule protects your reputation.

Your sixth helper gives you a weekly ad insight report. Meta cost per lead is useful, but the better question is which campaign turns into booked tests and closed installs. This helper compares ad spend, leads, appointments, shows, quotes, installs, and revenue. It can recommend what to scale, hold, or pause, but you approve budget changes. The helper advises. You decide.

The safe rollout is small. Week one, launch speed to lead for new Meta leads. Week two, start aged lead reactivation in controlled waves. Week three, add appointment reminders and quote follow up. Week four, add reviews, referrals, and ad insight reporting. Each Friday, check what happened. If a helper saves time and stays accurate, keep it. If it creates confusion, narrow the job and try again.
