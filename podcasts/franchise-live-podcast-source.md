# Franchise LIVE podcast build

Open the audio with EXACTLY these words:

"Hi there, welcome. This walkthrough was built for you and Franchise LIVE, LLC, from what you told us."

Use direct second-person language. Say you and your often. Do not use analyst framing. Do not mention internal gates. Include the bare qualifier link only: https://bennett-maxwell.github.io/fki-preview/qualify.html

Spoken script begins below.

Hi there, welcome. This walkthrough was built for you and Franchise LIVE, LLC, from what you told us.

You get a cleaner way to see what is happening. Your team gets clearer ownership. You can check each helper by the receipts it leaves, and you can pause any helper that is not making your work easier.

Franchise LIVE has a clear job to do: bring franchise brands, buyers, and interested operators into a more useful live and on demand experience. The opportunity is not to make one giant AI system. The opportunity is to give each repeated job a narrow helper with a scoreboard, guardrails, and a weekly improvement loop.

The first helper is the audience seat agent. Each episode has limited live seats, so every request needs a status. This helper can capture seat requests, confirm attendance, manage the waitlist, and route overflow to the next best option. It should never promise a seat that is not available. It should show the episode, seat count, request time, and confirmation status for every action.

The second helper is the production coordinator. A monthly episode has guests, assets, reminders, recordings, edits, approvals, and publishing steps. This helper watches the checklist, chases missing assets, flags delays, and creates a daily summary. It does not approve final creative or publish on its own. It keeps the pipeline visible so fewer details fall through the cracks.

The third helper is the membership growth agent. People who watch, register, or ask questions need a clear next step. This helper can welcome new members, remind them about available episodes, win back quiet members, and summarize engagement patterns. It should use approved copy and route unusual questions to a person. The weekly scoreboard is signups, active members, watched episodes, replies, and cancellations saved.

The fourth helper is the brand partnership agent. Franchise brands need a clear pitch process and clean expectations. This helper can organize brand inquiries, gather fit signals, prepare pitch-slot notes, and draft follow up. It never signs a brand deal, changes pricing, or promises placement. It gives your team a clean recommendation with the facts behind it.

The fifth helper is the buyer recruitment agent. Qualified franchise buyers and investors need timely invitations and reminders. This helper can segment interest signals, invite the right people to relevant episodes, confirm attendance, and report which messages bring serious buyers. It should not make earnings claims or investment promises. Anything financial or legal goes to a human.

The sixth helper is the post episode follow up agent. After each episode, the work is not finished. This helper sends approved recaps, collects feedback, routes warm buyer or brand conversations, and reminds the team about open next steps. It should show who attended, what they clicked or asked, and what follow up is due next.

The safest rollout is small. Week one, launch audience seat management and production coordination. Week two, add post episode follow up. Week three, add membership growth. Week four, add brand partnership and buyer recruitment. Each Friday, review the scoreboard and decide which helper earned more trust.

The fifth-grade version is this: Franchise LIVE can have six careful helpers. One fills seats. One keeps the show on schedule. One helps members come back. One helps brands get organized. One invites the right buyers. One follows up after each episode. People still make the big decisions. The helpers make sure the repeated work gets done.

Every helper needs receipts. A seat helper should show the requested episode and seat status. A production helper should show which checklist item is late. A membership helper should show which member action triggered the message. A brand helper should show the fit signals. A buyer helper should show why that buyer was invited. A follow up helper should show the episode, attendee, and next step.

This fits the AI agents lesson from the video. The value is not a chatbot that talks about everything. The value is a worker with a job. A worker needs inputs, actions, guardrails, a scoreboard, and a way to improve. When each helper owns one job, the system gets safer and easier to manage.

Your best first move is audience seats plus production coordination. Those jobs are close to the core experience and easy to measure. If live seats get filled more cleanly and production steps stop slipping, the system proves itself quickly. Then follow up, membership, brand partnership, and buyer recruitment can be added one at a time.

The important safety rule is simple. AI can draft, sort, remind, summarize, and recommend. Humans approve brand deals, pricing, legal claims, franchise disclosure questions, investment claims, final publication, and any message that could create a commitment. That line keeps the helpers useful without letting them make risky promises.

If this is working, the team should see fewer missed follow ups, cleaner episode prep, better attendance visibility, better member engagement, and clearer brand and buyer pipelines. The weekly report should show what happened, what is blocked, and which helper needs a tighter rule. That is how the system improves without guessing.

So the plan is simple: fill seats, protect production, grow membership, organize brand interest, invite qualified buyers, and follow up after every episode. Start with one or two helpers, prove them with receipts, then add the next. That is the practical path from AI idea to AI workforce. Keep the first helper narrow, prove it with a weekly scorecard, and only then trust it with more responsibility.
