Hi Jason, welcome. This walkthrough was built for you and Peak Comfort HVAC, from what you told us. You run an HVAC and plumbing business where speed, trust, and scheduling clarity matter. When a homeowner has no heat, weak cooling, a leaking water heater, or a plumbing problem, the team that responds clearly and quickly has the advantage. The goal of this AI plan is not to replace your team. The goal is to give your team helpers that collect details, draft messages, and flag the jobs that need human attention. The first helper is the Speed-to-Lead Agent. When a new call, form, or message comes in, it drafts a fast response, asks for service address, system type, urgency, photos if useful, and preferred appointment windows. It routes emergencies, safety issues, and price questions to a human. You track response time, qualified leads, booked calls, and exceptions every day. The second helper is the Dispatch and Scheduling Agent. It turns customer information into a cleaner job packet for the dispatcher and technician. It can draft ETA updates, reschedule options, and job-status messages, but it does not promise time slots or emergency availability unless your calendar rules allow it. You track schedule changes, confirmations, technician handoff quality, and customer replies. The third helper is the Proposal Generator. It uses human-entered notes, photos, equipment details, and service recommendations to draft a plain-English proposal summary. It never invents price, labor, code, warranty, rebate, or parts language. A manager approves the final version before a customer sees it. You track proposal speed, revision rate, close rate, and manager-approved exceptions. The fourth helper is the Content Production Agent. Every week your team sees real questions from homeowners: when to replace a filter, what a strange sound means, why a tune-up matters, what to do before summer, or when to stop using a leaking system. The content agent turns those safe topics into review-ready posts, emails, and FAQ answers. Humans approve anything public, technical, or safety related. The fifth helper is the Maintenance Recall Agent. Past HVAC and plumbing customers often need reminders before the next season or before a small issue becomes an emergency. This agent looks at completed jobs and drafts recall messages using approved timing rules. It stops for complaints, warranty concerns, or do-not-contact flags. You track booked tune-ups, repeat jobs, opt-outs, and revenue recovered. The sixth helper is the Review and Reputation Agent. After a completed service call, it drafts a thank-you and review request. If the customer replies happily, it routes them to the review link. If they mention a problem, it stops and alerts a manager. For public reviews, it drafts responses but never posts without approval. You track reviews earned, negative replies caught early, and response time. The weekly rhythm should be simple. Monday, review speed-to-lead results, open estimates, and maintenance recall opportunities. During the week, dispatch and review drafts run with safety gates. Friday, read one page: leads answered, jobs booked, estimates followed up, tune-ups recalled, reviews requested, complaints escalated, and the three decisions your humans need to make next. The safety rules are the guardrails. Agents can draft, summarize, classify, and remind. Humans approve prices, emergency language, warranty claims, code language, schedule promises, public review replies, and any message that could affect customer trust.
