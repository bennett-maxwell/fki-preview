## 3. The market reality

Commercial kitchen exhaust cleaning is not just cleaning. Buyers care about fire risk, inspection readiness, health and fire marshal confidence, insurance records, downtime, scheduling, and proof. In California, kitchen exhaust cleaning expectations are tied to NFPA 96 and Title 19 enforcement patterns, and local authorities can add stricter expectations.

Competitors in Southern California already lean into documented compliance. Kitchen Guard Orange County emphasizes NFPA 96-compliant cleaning, before-and-after photos, and service reports. SoCal Hoods positions around Orange County commercial kitchens and NFPA 96 compliance. This means Surfline should not only say it cleans hoods; it should operationalize proof, reminders, and documentation.

## 4. The highest-leverage first AI employee: Kitchen Quote Intake Agent

Start with the Kitchen Quote Intake Agent. Its job is to capture every inquiry from the website, email, text, missed calls, and uploaded photos. It asks for missing job details: city, kitchen type, hood count, access notes, urgency, inspection deadline, whether there are fan or duct issues, and preferred service windows.

For MJ, this is especially valuable because Surfline is early. A new lead cannot sit for hours, and a vague form submission cannot become a clean quote. The agent creates a job packet, routes it to MJ, and tracks whether the quote is complete. Anything customer-facing can be drafted first and approved before sending.

## 5. Compliance Cadence Agent

The second employee is the Compliance Cadence Agent. It tracks last service date, next recommended cleaning window, photos, certificates or proof folders, customer location, kitchen type, and reminders. The finish line is simple: every customer has a next-service reminder and a proof folder.

Do not overclaim certification. The agent should not invent compliance language or final certification wording. It can organize evidence, draft reminders, and prepare reports for review. MJ approves anything that represents the company in compliance terms.

## 6. Local SEO Content Agent

MJ specifically asked about SEO and website development. Surfline needs city and service-area content that is useful and compliance-safe. The Local SEO Content Agent drafts pages and posts for searches like Orange County commercial hood cleaning, Los Angeles hood cleaning, San Bernardino commercial kitchen exhaust cleaning, fan repair, filter replacement, food truck hood cleaning, and emergency hood repair.

This agent does not publish automatically. It drafts title tags, FAQs, service area copy, Google Business Profile posts, and internal link ideas. MJ reviews before publishing. It can also flag website issues like the San Bernardino spelling typo and missing photo proof sections.

## 7. Before and After Proof Agent

The Before and After Proof Agent turns technician photos and notes into clean proof packets. It checks whether each job has pre-cleaning photos, post-cleaning photos, fan or rooftop pictures when relevant, filter shots, notes, and a summary the customer can understand.

This turns completed work into trust assets. Proof packets help customers, future buyers, website content, Google Business Profile updates, and internal QA. Every send should be reviewed, but the organizing work can be automated.

## 8. Repair Opportunity Agent

Surfline does more than clean. The website lists fans, ducts, filters, and hood repairs. The Repair Opportunity Agent reads job notes and photos, flags repair opportunities, drafts recommended next steps, and creates a follow-up task. It never diagnoses beyond approved rules, and it never sends pricing without MJ approval.

This matters because cleaning jobs can reveal fan issues, filter problems, duct damage, or follow-up maintenance. The agent makes sure those opportunities do not disappear in a technician note or text thread.

## 9. Commercial Kitchen Outreach Agent

The outreach agent builds lists of target commercial kitchens and facility teams, not spam blasts. It can segment by geography, category, urgency, and service fit. It drafts short, compliance-safe introductions that MJ approves before sending.

Because MJ understands systems, this can be a CRM workflow from day one: target list, status, last touch, next touch, proof asset, objection, and booked inspection or quote request. The agent should log everything and keep the tone practical, not hypey.

## 10. What the website should become

The website should become a conversion and trust machine. Add structured quote intake, photo upload, service area pages, before-and-after proof, clear service categories, an FAQ around cleaning cadence and inspection documentation, and a stronger proof section.

A smart first version would include: Orange County page, Los Angeles County page, San Bernardino County page, hood cleaning page, fan repair page, filter replacement page, emergency service page, food truck page, and compliance-support page. Each page should be careful: no unverified license or certification claims, no fake reviews, no fabricated customer logos.

## 11. Operating system for Surfline

The AI stack can start simple: website form, email, SMS or phone transcripts, CRM, Google Drive job folders, calendar, and Google Business Profile drafts. Every agent should have a human approval boundary. The system should track response time, quote packet completeness, reminders created, proof reports completed, repair follow-ups created, and SEO drafts reviewed.

MJ does not need a giant system on day one. He needs one tight workflow that compounds. Start with intake, then compliance cadence, then proof reports, then SEO, then repair follow-up, then outreach.
