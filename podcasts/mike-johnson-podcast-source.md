## Talk track

Mike, your plumbing company wins when calls are answered fast, job details are captured cleanly, estimates are followed up, customers know the next step, and the office has enough proof to make good decisions. AI should not replace your judgment. It should prepare the work so you and your team can move faster with fewer dropped balls.

The first helper is a missed-call recovery agent. It watches calls, form fills, and after-hours messages, then drafts a response for review. It asks one useful question, names the plumbing issue, and gives the next safe step. It never promises price, arrival window, or emergency availability without approval.

The second helper is a new customer intake agent. It gathers the service address, job type, urgency, shutoff status, photos, preferred appointment window, and contact details. It keeps the office from chasing the same missing information over and over.

The third helper is an estimate follow-up agent. It tracks open estimates and drafts follow-ups that are specific, respectful, and grounded in the job notes. It can remind a customer about a water heater option, a leak repair, or a drain cleaning recommendation without inventing discounts or pressure.

The fourth helper is a review request agent. It only asks for a public review when the job is complete and there is no unresolved complaint. If something is still open, it drafts an internal follow-up instead.

The fifth helper is a job summary agent. It turns rough technician notes into a customer-friendly summary, an office summary, a next-step recommendation, and a proof checklist.

The sixth helper is an admin triage agent. It sorts routine questions and escalates sensitive work. Anything involving money, warranty, legal wording, safety risk, angry customers, credentials, or public claims goes to a human.

The weekly scoreboard should stay simple: missed calls recovered, intake packets completed, estimates followed up, reviews requested, job summaries created, and escalations caught before they became problems.
