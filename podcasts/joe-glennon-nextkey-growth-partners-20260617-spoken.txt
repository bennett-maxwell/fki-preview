The implementation order matters. Start with the workflow closest to revenue or response time. Do not start with a big automation map. Start with one repeatable moment where a message arrives, gets understood, gets summarized, and gets a useful next step.

Days one through three should be about workflow capture. Confirm where inquiries arrive, what information is usually missing, who approves sensitive replies, and what a successful first response looks like.

Days four through seven should be about a controlled pilot. Use internal examples first, then real conversations only after the language and escalation rules are clear. Measure whether the agent improves speed, quality, and handoff clarity.

By day thirty, the goal is a repeatable rhythm: review the conversations, update the prompts, document the exceptions, and decide which adjacent workflow deserves the next agent.
