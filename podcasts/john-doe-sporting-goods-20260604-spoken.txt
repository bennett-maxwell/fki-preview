Hi John, welcome. This walkthrough was built for you and Doe's Sporting Goods, from what you told us. You run a specialty retail business where timing matters. A customer looking at gear today may be comparing options online, checking local inventory, or deciding whether to come into the store. The goal of this AI plan is not to replace your team. The goal is to give your team a set of helpers that remember follow-ups, watch inventory signals, and prepare customer messages for human approval. The first agent is the Abandoned Cart Recovery Agent. When someone leaves gear in a Shopify cart, the agent drafts a helpful follow-up that mentions the actual product category. A baseball glove message should not sound like a fishing rod message. High-value carts get routed to your team for personal follow-up. Small carts get a simple reminder and then stop. You track recovered carts, replies, opt-outs, and dollars saved. The second agent is the Loyalty Program Agent. It reads Square and Shopify purchase history, groups customers by spending level, and drafts re-engagement messages for the right category. A runner gets different follow-up than a parent buying team sports equipment. You approve offers, discount rules, and message templates. The agent tracks repeat purchases, loyalty tier movement, and which messages actually bring people back. The third agent is the Inventory Alert Agent. Every morning, it checks item levels, recent sales, and reorder thresholds. It can flag fast-moving gear, slow movers, and items that may need markdowns before they take up too much shelf space. It does not place orders by itself. It prepares a buying brief for you: what is low, what is moving, what is stale, and what deserves a human decision. The fourth agent is the Review and Referral Agent. Thirty-six hours after a purchase, it drafts a short thank-you and review request when the customer had a normal experience. If there is a return, complaint, warranty problem, or unhappy reply, the agent stops and routes it to a human. That keeps the store voice personal and safe. You track reviews earned, negative replies caught early, referrals created, and templates that need improvement. The fifth agent is the Customer Win-Back Agent. It finds people who have not purchased recently and drafts useful messages tied to their past category. Someone who bought soccer gear may need a seasonal reminder. Someone who bought camping equipment may care about a new local outdoor season. The agent never invents discounts or fake urgency. It uses only offers you approve, then tracks replies and repeat purchases. The sixth agent is the In-Store Upsell Briefing Agent. Each morning, it gives your team simple notes for high-value or returning customers: what they bought before, what category they like, what complementary item may make sense, and what not to push. This is not pressure selling. It is preparation. Your team still chooses what to say. The agent saves research time and helps staff make more useful recommendations. The weekly rhythm should be easy. Monday, you review inventory alerts and win-back opportunities. During the week, abandoned cart and review drafts run in the background with safety rules. Friday, you read one page: recovered carts, reviews earned, customers reactivated, low-stock alerts, suggested markdowns, and the three decisions that need your attention. The safety rules are simple. Agents prepare, draft, summarize, and flag. Humans approve discounts, public replies, buying decisions, sensitive customer messages, and anything that could affect customer trust. Every correction from you becomes better instructions for the next week. That is how the system gets smarter without becoming reckless. Add inventory and upsell briefings after the team trusts the data. In thirty days, Doe's Sporting Goods should have fewer missed follow-ups, clearer buying decisions, and a simple weekly scoreboard. It needs to recover carts, remind customers, watch shelves, prepare notes, and show proof every week. If the abandoned cart messages recover sales and the review requests earn good feedback, you can add the next helper with confidence. The point is a steady store system, not a flashy demo. Every week, the scoreboard should tell you what the helpers did, what humans approved, what customers liked, and what needs correction before the next week starts.  Start the scoreboard with only six numbers: carts recovered, reviews requested, reviews earned, inactive customers contacted, inventory alerts reviewed, and staff recommendations used.
