## Talk track

RJ, Kitchen Guard wins when service requests are answered quickly, site details are captured cleanly, technician notes turn into useful records, and recurring maintenance follow-up happens without creating risky promises. AI should prepare the work so you and your team can move faster. It should not make compliance claims or protected decisions.

The first helper is missed-call recovery. It drafts a response when a facility manager, restaurant operator, or kitchen team reaches out about hood cleaning, filters, fans, ducts, access, or follow-up service. It asks one useful question and routes anything sensitive to a human.

The second helper is customer intake. It gathers site address, contact, facility type, service category, access notes, photos, timing, and urgency.

The third helper is estimate follow-up. It watches open service opportunities and drafts respectful follow-ups grounded in the actual notes.

The fourth helper is service summaries. It turns rough technician notes into customer-friendly summaries, internal summaries, recommendations, and proof checklists.

The fifth helper is maintenance reminders. It prepares reminders tied to real service history without alarmist compliance claims.

The sixth helper is admin triage. It sorts routine questions and escalates price, warranty, compliance, legal language, angry customers, credentials, public claims, and irreversible changes.
