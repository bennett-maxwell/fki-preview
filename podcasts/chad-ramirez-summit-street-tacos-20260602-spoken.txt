Hi Chad, welcome. This walkthrough was built for you and Summit Street Tacos, from what you told us.

You run a fast casual taco group with catering demand, lunch rush pressure, loyalty marketing, and franchise curiosity hitting at the same time. Your best AI plan is a set of small supervised employees that each own one repeatable job.

First, your catering speed agent watches every inquiry and drafts the first helpful reply. It asks for date, headcount, service style, budget range, timing, dietary needs, and delivery details. You keep price and capacity approval. The agent keeps speed from depending on whoever happens to be free during lunch.

Second, your franchise inquiry agent qualifies expansion interest safely. It gathers market, capital range, restaurant experience, timeline, motivation, and operating partner status. It does not send legal paperwork. It does not make earnings claims. It gives you a clean summary so you choose the next step.

Third, your guest recovery agent reviews public reviews, direct messages, loyalty replies, and manager notes every morning. It sorts each issue by severity and drafts a response for approval. Safety, allergy, employee, refund, and media issues escalate before any reply leaves your team.

Fourth, your manager summary agent turns scattered store updates into one dashboard. It captures sales blockers, labor gaps, inventory alerts, catering opportunities, guest incidents, and open tasks. You get person, deadline, impact, and next action instead of chasing three locations for the same update.

Fifth, your local marketing agent drafts weekly promos for each store. It uses events, weather, slow periods, loyalty signals, catering goals, and neighborhood patterns. It marks every campaign as draft until you approve. Then it reports clicks, redemptions, catering leads, and repeatable winners.

Sixth, your weekly owner dashboard agent rolls everything into one Friday review. It separates approve, reject, watch, and delegate. That rhythm matters because your AI system should reduce scattered information, not create another inbox for you to manage.

Here is the simple safety rule. The agent does the repetitive work. You keep judgment, approvals, pricing, legal boundaries, refunds, hiring, firing, and brand decisions. Every agent needs a receipt, a metric, an owner, and a clear escalation path.

Your first thirty days should stay practical. Week one connects intake, catering messages, manager notes, and review monitoring. Week two tests drafts against real examples. Week three adds scorecards. Week four decides which tasks earned more trust.

If an agent cannot prove value, pause that workflow and correct the setup. If an agent saves time, protects revenue, or helps guests faster, hand it more of the repeatable work. That is how Summit Street Tacos can improve without adding chaos.

You do not need a giant transformation project. You need six focused AI employees, each with one job, one scorecard, one approval line, and one weekly review. That gives you leverage while keeping control in your hands.
