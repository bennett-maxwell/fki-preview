FDD Review Checklist — What to Look for Before You Sign a Franchise Agreement

The Franchise Disclosure Document is a legally mandated 23-item document that every franchisor must provide to prospective buyers at least 14 days before signing. Most people treat it like a terms-of-service agreement — they skim it, trust their excitement, and sign.

This is a mistake. The FDD is the single most important document in your franchise evaluation process, and knowing where to look separates informed investors from expensive lessons.

The Non-Negotiable Items

Item 3 — Litigation History

This item discloses all pending and past litigation involving the franchisor, its principals, and its affiliates. Look for:
- Pattern litigation (multiple franchisees suing for the same reason)
- Regulatory actions from state franchise authorities
- Judgments or settlements that indicate systemic franchisor failures

One lawsuit means nothing. A pattern of franchisee-initiated litigation is a red flag.

Item 19 — Financial Performance Representations

Not all franchisors are required to include Item 19, but most serious brands do. This is the data that tells you what franchisees actually earn.

#### How to Read Item 19

- Gross revenue is not owner income. Find the net or EBITDA figures.
- Average is misleading if the distribution is skewed. Look at median and quartile breakdowns.
- Mature unit data (3+ years in operation) is more relevant than system-wide averages that include ramp-up units.
- What's excluded — royalties, marketing fund contributions, local advertising spend. Make sure you're comparing apples to apples.

Item 20 — Outlets and Franchisee Information

This item tells you how many units opened, closed, transferred, or terminated in the last three years. High termination and transfer rates relative to new openings signal a struggling system.

The item also contains a full list of current and former franchisees with contact information. This is your validation call list.

Secondary Items to Review Carefully

Item 8 — Restrictions on Sources of Products and Services

Required vendor relationships affect your cost structure. Understand what you must buy from approved suppliers, at what price premium, and whether there are rebates flowing back to the franchisor.

Item 12 — Territory Rights

- Is your territory exclusive or protected?
- Under what conditions can the franchisor open a competing unit or sell through alternative channels (digital, wholesale) in your territory?
- What happens to your territory if you miss a performance threshold?

Item 17 — Renewal, Termination, and Transfer Rights

This is where the long-term risk lives. Review:
- Renewal conditions (do you sign a new agreement at updated terms?)
- Termination triggers (what can get you terminated without cure rights?)
- Transfer approval process and fees
- Right of first refusal on resale

The FDD Review Process

Step 1: Build Your Own Summary Sheet

For each FDD you receive, create a one-page summary of Items 3, 5, 6, 12, 17, 19, and 20. Standardize the format so you can compare multiple franchises side by side.

Step 2: Run Your Franchisee Validation Calls

Use the Item 20 list to identify franchisees in markets similar to your target territory. Call 8-12 franchisees across different tenure levels. Focus on financial reality vs. Item 19 projections.

Step 3: Engage a Franchise Attorney

A franchise attorney reviews the legal terms — not the financial logic. Hire one who specializes in franchise law (not a general business attorney) to review Items 9, 12, 17, and 22 before you sign.
