Franchise Broker vs Consultant — What's the Difference and Why It Matters

Most people use "franchise broker" and "franchise consultant" interchangeably. They are not the same thing, and the difference has real financial implications for the person writing the check.

Understanding who is paying your advisor — and what they're incentivized to recommend — is the first step to protecting yourself during franchise selection.

What Is a Franchise Broker?

A franchise broker represents franchisors. They are paid a referral fee (typically 40-50% of the franchise fee, often $12,000-$25,000 per placement) when you sign a franchise agreement with one of the brands in their portfolio.

The Broker Model's Built-In Conflict

The broker's financial incentive is to match you to a brand that pays a referral fee — not necessarily the brand that's the best fit for your situation. Most brokers work with a curated portfolio of 100-300 brands. Opportunities outside their network are invisible to you unless you find them independently.

This doesn't make brokers dishonest. It makes them structurally limited.

When a Broker Is Appropriate

Franchise brokers add value when you want introductions to established brands, need help with the discovery day process, and are working with a consultant who also provides independent scoring.

What Is a Franchise Consultant?

A franchise consultant advises the buyer. In a pure consulting model, they are paid by you — not the franchisor — which means their recommendations are not filtered by referral relationships.

What Independent Consulting Looks Like

A true franchise consultant:
- Evaluates any brand in the market, not just brands in a referral network
- Scores opportunities against your specific financial and lifestyle criteria
- Reviews FDD data without a financial stake in which brand you choose
- Provides financial modeling, territory analysis, and franchisee validation support

The Difference in Recommendations

A broker's shortlist is filtered by what brands pay referral fees and what's available in their network. A consultant's shortlist is filtered by what scores highest against your profile.

The Hybrid Reality

Many advisors in the franchise industry operate as broker-consultants — they provide genuine advisory services but also earn referral fees. This model can work, but the buyer needs to understand how the advisor is compensated and which brands are in-network vs. out-of-network.

Questions to Ask Any Advisor

- How are you compensated? By the buyer, the franchisor, or both?
- How many brands are you able to recommend?
- Do you have an exclusivity or referral relationship with any of the brands on my shortlist?
- What happens to your compensation if I choose a brand outside your portfolio?

If your advisor is paid by the franchisor, you are not their client — you are their product. Understand the incentive structure before you trust the recommendation.
