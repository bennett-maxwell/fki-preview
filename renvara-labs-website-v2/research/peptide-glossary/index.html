Peptide testing glossary

Plain definitions of the terms on a certificate of analysis, from analyzed date to sterility test.

Shop the catalog (opens in a new tab)Open the COA library

By Renvara Labs. Published 2026-10-10. Sources are listed at the end. For laboratory research use only.

How to use this glossary

This glossary defines the 31 terms most often found on a peptide certificate of analysis or in the explanations on this site. Terms are in alphabetical order. Each definition is general background, not a statement about a particular lot. For a specific lot, always read the original laboratory PDF and match its lot number to your vial.

Terms, A to Z

Show the list: Terms, A to Z
Amino acid
A small organic molecule that is a building block of peptides and proteins. A peptide is written as an ordered list of amino acids.
Analyzed date
The date the laboratory tested the sample. On Renvara's six public reports it is 2026-09-16.
Blend
A product that combines more than one peptide. A report for a blend lists each component with its own purity and measured quantity.
Catalog code
The short label a seller uses for a product. A catalog code is not a chemical name. Renvara lists retatrutide under the catalog code GLP3-R.
Certificate of analysis (COA)
A document in which a laboratory records the methods and results for a specific sample or lot. It describes that sample, not every lot of a product.
Chromatogram
The plot produced by liquid chromatography. Each peak is a component of the sample; peak area is used to calculate purity.
Counterion
An ion that balances the charge of a peptide. After preparative reversed-phase HPLC, peptides are usually delivered as trifluoroacetate (TFA) salts.
Elemental impurities
Trace metals and metalloids, such as lead, arsenic, cadmium and mercury, measured by methods such as ICP-MS.
Endotoxin
A lipopolysaccharide from the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria. It is measured with the LAL test and reported in endotoxin units (EU).
HPLC
High-performance liquid chromatography. A method that separates the components of a sample on a column so each can be detected and measured.
Hygroscopic
Tending to absorb moisture from the air. Many dry peptides are hygroscopic, which is why storage guidance stresses tightly closed vials and dry conditions.
INN (International Nonproprietary Name)
The generic name assigned to a substance by the World Health Organization. Retatrutide, tesamorelin and similar names are INNs.
LAL (Limulus amebocyte lysate)
A reagent that reacts to bacterial endotoxin. LAL-based tests are described in USP chapter <85>.
Lot (batch) number
The identifier printed on a vial label that links the vial to the production lot, and therefore to the report that covers that lot.
Lyophilized
Freeze-dried: water has been removed from a frozen sample under vacuum, leaving a dry solid.
Mass spectrometry (MS)
A method that measures the mass of molecules after they are turned into ions. It supports confirmation of identity by comparing observed and expected mass.
Mass-to-charge ratio (m/z)
The quantity a mass spectrometer measures for each ion. Software converts a set of m/z values into one observed molecular mass.
Measured quantity
The amount of the target compound a laboratory reports finding in the tested sample, given in mass units such as milligrams. Compare it with the labeled strength.
Molecular formula
The count of each kind of atom in a molecule, such as C62H98N16O22 for BPC-157 in PubChem.
Molecular weight
The mass of a molecule in daltons or grams per mole. Databases such as PubChem list a computed value for each compound record.
Net peptide content
The fraction of a dry peptide sample that is peptide by weight. The rest is typically water and counterions. It differs from chromatographic purity.
Peptide
A short chain of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. The boundary between a peptide and a protein is conventional, not exact.
Purity (chromatographic)
The main peak's area as a percentage of total peak area in a chromatogram. It is an area percent, not a weight percent.
Quantitation
The measurement of how much of a compound is present, as distinct from identity or purity.
Research use only (RUO)
A label meaning the material is supplied for laboratory research and is not for human or animal use, diagnostic use or use in food.
Residual solvent
An organic solvent left over from synthesis or purification, measured against limits such as those in USP <467> and ICH Q3C.
Retention time
How long a component takes to travel through a chromatography column. It helps identify the main peak in a chromatogram.
Sequence
The order of amino acids in a peptide, written from the N-terminus to the C-terminus.
Specification
A stated limit or acceptance criterion that a result is compared against, such as a maximum endotoxin level.
Sterility test
A test for viable bacteria and fungi in a sample, with incubation in growth media, described in USP chapter <71>.
UPLC
Ultra-performance liquid chromatography. Liquid chromatography with smaller particles and higher pressure, giving sharper separations in less time.
