How to read a peptide certificate of analysis

Ten things to find on a COA, one worked example from a public lot, and the mix-ups that cause the most confusion.

Shop the catalog (opens in a new tab)Open the COA library

By Renvara Labs. Published 2026-10-10. Sources are listed at the end. For laboratory research use only.

The short version

A peptide certificate of analysis describes one lot. To read it, match the product, strength and lot number to your vial, then find the laboratory, the analyzed date, the method, and the result lines with their units. Check what the report does not cover, and open the original PDF on the laboratory’s own site.

Ten things to find on a certificate

Show the list: Ten things to find on a certificate
  1. Product name. It should match the name on your vial. If a catalog code is used, check that the code maps to the compound (for example, Renvara lists retatrutide under the catalog code GLP3-R).
  2. Labeled strength. The amount per vial that the label claims, for example 10 mg.
  3. Lot or batch number. The single most important match. A report for a different lot does not describe your vial.
  4. Analyzed date. When the laboratory tested the sample. Renvara’s six public reports show 2026-09-16.
  5. Laboratory and report number. Who issued the report, and a number you can quote to support. Renvara’s reports name Horizon Analytical.
  6. Method. Renvara’s library lists UPLC/MS. A method tells you what the result can and cannot show.
  7. Results and units. Purity is usually a percentage of peak area. A measured quantity is usually a mass such as milligrams.
  8. For blends, one line per component. A two- or three-peptide product should show a result for each peptide, not a single combined number.
  9. The chromatogram or spectrum. If the PDF includes one, look for a single dominant peak and the expected mass.
  10. Scope. A report covers only the tests it lists. A purity result is not an endotoxin, sterility, metals or solvents result.

Worked example: lot DPS-6373036

Lot DPS-6373036 is listed as retatrutide, catalog code GLP3-R, 10 mg labeled. Horizon Analytical report 1028837 was analyzed on 2026-09-16 and lists UPLC/MS purity, identity and quantitation. The laboratory reports 99.72% purity and a measured quantity of 9.91 mg against the 10 mg label.

Read the full section: Worked example: lot DPS-6373036

Reading it step by step: the product, strength and lot match what a vial with that lot would carry. The date and laboratory are shown. The result is two numbers, a purity percentage and a measured quantity, and the second is compared with the labeled strength. Nothing on the library listing says the sample was tested for endotoxin, sterility, metals or solvents, so a reader should not infer those results.

Common mix-ups

Show the list: Common mix-ups
  • Purity is not content. A high purity percentage describes the share of the chromatogram, not the share of the powder that is peptide. See purity vs net peptide content.
  • One lot is not every lot. Renvara’s Terms say not to assume that a report for one lot applies to another.
  • The brand field can differ. On the three reports opened in the 2026-10-10 audit, the brand field reads “peptides” rather than Renvara Labs. Match by product, strength and lot number.
  • A pass statement is not a data table. Prefer a report that shows the numbers and the method over one that only states a conclusion.
